
FINAL CONCLUSION
================

The experiment developed and evaluated supervised Naive Bayes classifiers
for predicting the predefined customer segments A, B, C, and D using
Dataset A.

Four core models were compared using the same five-fold cross-validation
procedure. CategoricalNB achieved the highest mean CV Macro F1 of
0.4845 with a fold-level SD of
0.0101. The combined representation
containing all nine predictors was selected.

The selected CategoricalNB model was then trained on the complete training
set and evaluated once on the locked test set. The final test accuracy was
0.5118, Macro F1 was 0.4853, and weighted F1 was
0.4955.

Class-wise analysis showed that Segment B was the most difficult class,
while Segment D had the strongest classification performance. The largest
confusion pattern was B -> C, followed by B -> A.

The results demonstrate that Naive Bayes can provide a useful baseline for
mixed-type customer segment prediction, but the moderate Macro F1 and
unequal class-wise performance indicate that predictions should be used
carefully.

The model should be treated as decision support rather than as a definitive
description of individual customers. Privacy protection, fairness
monitoring, human review, label provenance, and periodic reassessment are
important before considering operational use.
