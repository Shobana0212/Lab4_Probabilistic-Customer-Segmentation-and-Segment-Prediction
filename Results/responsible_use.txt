
RESPONSIBLE USE, PRIVACY, FAIRNESS AND LIMITATIONS
==================================================

1. PRIVACY
Customer profiling can involve sensitive demographic and behavioral
information. Customer IDs are used only for traceability and are not
included as predictors. Direct identifiers should be removed before model
training or deployment. Access to customer-level predictions should be
restricted to authorized users.

2. FAIRNESS
The model performance is not uniform across the four segments. Segment B
has substantially lower recall and F1 than Segments C and D. Therefore,
aggregate accuracy alone should not be used to judge model quality.
Subgroup performance should be monitored before any operational deployment.

3. STEREOTYPING AND PROFILING
Demographic variables such as age, gender, marital status, graduation,
profession, and family size may encode social or economic differences.
Predictions should not be interpreted as statements about a person's
identity, worth, ability, or behavior. The model predicts the predefined
business label; it does not discover an objective or inherent customer type.

4. MISUSE RISK
Predicted segments should not be used as the sole basis for decisions that
could unfairly restrict access, pricing, employment, credit, essential
services, or other opportunities. Human review should be available for
uncertain or business-critical cases.

5. CONFIDENCE
Naive Bayes posterior probabilities represent model confidence under the
model assumptions. They should not be treated as certainty about a
customer's true segment.

6. MODEL LIMITATIONS
Naive Bayes assumes conditional independence among predictors given the
class. This assumption may not hold for customer attributes. The model also
depends on the quality, representativeness, and provenance of the supplied
dataset and its predefined labels.

7. LABEL LIMITATION
The target Segmentation label is an existing predefined business label.
The classifier learns to reproduce that label. It does not establish that
the four segments are naturally occurring customer groups.

8. ERROR HANDLING
Segment B has the weakest classification performance. The largest observed
error pattern is B -> C, followed by B -> A. These errors indicate that
some customers are difficult to distinguish using the available features.

9. HUMAN OVERSIGHT
Low-confidence predictions can be routed for review using the frozen
validation-derived review threshold. Human review should be used for
uncertain or consequential cases rather than treating automated predictions
as unquestionable decisions.

10. BUSINESS RECOMMENDATION
Use the model primarily as decision support for aggregate customer analysis.
Monitor class-wise performance, review uncertain cases, periodically audit
data quality and label provenance, and reassess performance when the
customer population or business process changes.
